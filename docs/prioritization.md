# Documentation: US Legal Research Logic and Prioritization

## 1. The Jurisdictional Framework

US Law is divided into **Federal** and **State** systems. Research must strictly follow the jurisdiction where the case is being tried.

- **Mandatory (Binding) Authority:** Decisions from higher courts within the same jurisdiction that a lower court _must_ follow.
- **Persuasive Authority:** Decisions from other jurisdictions or lower courts that a judge _may_ consider but is not required to follow.

---

## 2. Court Hierarchy (The Vertical Rule)

Precedent flows downward. A higher court's decision is superior to a lower court's decision within the same system.

### A. State Court Hierarchy

1.  **State Supreme Court** (Highest)
2.  **Intermediate Appellate Court**
3.  **Trial/District Court** (Lowest)

### B. Federal Court Hierarchy

1.  **U.S. Supreme Court (SCOTUS)**
2.  **U.S. Circuit Courts of Appeals** (e.g., 6th Circuit)
3.  **U.S. District Courts** (e.g., Eastern District of Michigan)

---

## 3. The Priority Paradox: Factual Similarity vs. Rank

In practice, **Factual Similarity (Factually On-Point)** often overrides **Court Rank** during the research process.

| Factor            | Description                                                            | Priority      |
| :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------ |
| **Factual Match** | How closely the specific details of the case match the current matter. | **Primary**   |
| **Court Rank**    | The level of the court (Supreme vs. Appellate).                        | **Secondary** |
| **Jurisdiction**  | The geographic and legal authority of the court.                       | **Mandatory** |

**The Logic:** If a Supreme Court case is very old or involves different facts (e.g., a "hammer" vs. a "knife"), a more recent Appellate Court case involving the exact same facts (a "knife") is often more valuable and persuasive to a judge.

---

## 4. Operational Workflow for AI Research

When analyzing legal queries, follow these steps in order:

1.  **Identify Jurisdiction:** Determine if the case is Federal or State and pinpoint the specific geographic region.
2.  **Search for Factual Keywords:** Look for specific "fact-heavy" keywords rather than broad legal concepts (e.g., search for "dog bite on sidewalk" rather than "general negligence").
3.  **Establish Binding Hierarchy:** Locate the highest court in that jurisdiction that has ruled on the matter.
4.  **Verify Case Status (Good Law):** Use a citator to ensure the case has not been:
    - **Overruled:** No longer valid.
    - **Distinguished:** Found to be inapplicable due to different facts.
    - **Vacated:** Rendered void.

---

## 5. Practical Example

- **Target Case:** A person was injured by a **Knife**.
- **Precedent A (Supreme Court):** Involves a **Hammer**.
- **Precedent B (Appellate Court):** Involves a **Knife**.

**Research Strategy:** Prioritize **Precedent B**. Although it comes from a lower court than the Supreme Court, its "Factual Match" makes it a stronger "Binding Instruction" for the current judge to follow.
